Rich text

Reading as a form of attention

A magazine page should do more than carry words. It should slow the hand, soften the room, and make the reader feel that a considered human being has prepared the path ahead.

The useful silence around a sentence

Whitespace is not emptiness. In editorial design, it is pacing. It gives an image its hush, a heading its authority, and a paragraph enough air to become memorable.

Good typography does not perform confidence; it creates conditions for trust.

Editorial habits

  • Lead with rhythm before decoration.
  • Let metadata be useful without becoming noisy.
  • Reserve accent color for moments of real emphasis.

A link should feel like a promise, not an interruption. Inline code such as reading-time remains quiet and legible.

On keeping a room for unfinished thoughts

The best notebooks are not archives of certainty. They are rooms where a sentence may sit for months without being asked to explain itself.

In a culture that prizes the completed draft, there is a quiet defiance in maintaining a place for fragments: a line overheard at the market, the color of rain on brick, a question that seems more useful before it has an answer.

A practice of delay

To delay is not always to avoid. Sometimes it is a form of care. The unfinished thought keeps its edges porous enough to gather weather, conversation, and contradiction.

The page becomes a threshold: not the room itself, but the place where we decide how gently to enter.
  • Write the observation before the explanation.
  • Leave space around the line that makes you nervous.
  • Return when the sentence has cooled.
