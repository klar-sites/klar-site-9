Correspondence

Write to a person, not a department.

Send a response to an essay, propose a conversation, share a reading note, or simply leave a sentence at the door. Every message is read by the editor.

A small invitation

The best letters rarely arrive polished. They begin with something noticed: a line that stayed, a disagreement with a pulse, a question that has not yet found its room.

Editor’s desk

What belongs here

The Still Current is small by design. There is no submissions portal, no ticket number, no automated promise to “circle back.” If your note feels like correspondence, it belongs here.

You might write about

Responses. Tell us which essay followed you into the week, or where you found yourself arguing in the margin.

Conversations. Propose an interview, reading group, event, or quiet collaboration with a clear reason for its shape.

Letters. Send a brief dispatch from your own desk, table, train window, garden, or half-finished notebook.

Replies within a week when possible No PR blasts, please
Send a note

Begin anywhere.

A real endpoint can replace the placeholder action when the magazine is ready to receive mail.

By sending, you are simply starting a conversation. Nothing will be added to a mailing list unless you ask.

Correspondence is the old technology of taking another person’s attention seriously.

— The Still Current

No urgency. No daily noise. Just one careful note when there is something worth sending.
